Consider the paper at the link $0

Academic papers are usually built/motivated from/around a small number of core ideas, most often a singular atomic idea, and presented to include theoretical introduction, historic overview, state of art, idea development, presented context, results, benchmarks, application, rigorous proofs including all pre-proof and post-proof statements, wrapped into appropriate jargon and filler statements. Thus real neat eureka moment stays deeply buried and hidden behind all the folclore of the presentation; our aim is to bring that moment to the surface and expose it as clean and fresh as the very moment it happened.

At first glance we don't know the context of the moment; was it a briliant idea on how to solve long lasting problem by creatively novel or leveraging just encountered third-party technique, or the very problem being solved is itself the idea or task provided by external party attacked via well established tools, or was it a routine extension of a previous research activities, or a mere low-contribution publishing buildup of publish-or-perish style. Tasks below tend to approach the analysis from different angles.

0. State the most concise info: title, authors, date, abstract

1. Infer the most plausible single pre-technical epiphany that could have initiated the paper: the simplest speculative thought that would naturally generate the research program before its methods or results were known. Do not state the theorem, technical mechanism, proof idea, or completed-paper interpretation. Output one jargon-free sentence that could plausibly have appeared as the project’s first whiteboard note. 

2. Extract, in one jargon-free sentence, the minimal conceptual mechanism underlying the paper’s primary original contribution—not merely a proof trick or implementation detail; the sentence must explain the headline result, distinguish the work from prior art, and include every inseparable component of the idea, such as both an obstruction and the matching construction.

3. Sometimes that one core epiphany/idea did not spark in ones mind spotaneously without large prior context. Consider it is a continuation of long-running research by expert possessing comprehensive overview of the domain; infer a single thought passing through the author's mind triggering this work.

4. Considering authors' previous work, style of presentation, details of the content. Answer in one concise paragraph what was the most likely personal context, state of mind, and the source of the impulse to create the paper; was it genuine internal motivation of novel discovery, or external third party input or condition of most likely what exact nature?
